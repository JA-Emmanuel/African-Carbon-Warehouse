### 3. [Carbon Intensity of Growth: Which African Economies Are Growing Dirty? II](https://greenledgerafrica.substack.com/p/carbon-intensity-of-growth-which-3f5)
**Published:** Jun 26, 2026.
**Summary:** Carbon intensity of growth is the right variable for investment and policy decisions. But per capita emissions cannot be dismissed. It is where the equity argument lives, and any serious analysis of African emissions needs to hold both variables at the same time rather than choosing between them. Plotting fossil CO₂ per GDP against fossil CO₂ per capita produces a sharper picture than either variable alone. It separates countries where the economy is carbon-heavy from countries where the individual is carbon-heavy, and it reveals cases where both are true at once. Those cases are where the transition challenge is most acute, and where the policy implications are hardest to avoid. 
**Data used:** Was Done in Excel

### 4. [The Inverted Emitter: Nigeria's Sixty-Megatonne Transport Problem](https://greenledgerafrica.substack.com/p/the-inverted-emitter-nigerias-sixty)
**Published:** September 11, 2026.
**Summary:** 60 megatonnes. That's how much CO2 Nigeria's transport sector alone emitted last year, more than any other country in Africa. While power plants lead emissions in the larger emitting economies on the continent, in Nigeria it's the opposite. Transport makes up nearly half of all emissions. Power? Just 12%. It's not that Nigeria's grid is clean. It's that Nigeria's grid barely reaches everyone, so the emissions show up on the road instead of the power line. 
**Data used:** setoral_emissions
